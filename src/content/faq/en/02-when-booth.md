The real question underneath this one is: **can the interpreter speak audibly
inside the room?**

**You do not need a booth when:**

- up to 30 people are listening to interpretation;
- the room already has noise of its own (plant, site, moving group), or it is a
  small meeting;
- the group moves between areas.

In those cases the portable system covers it, and covers it for less — no
rigging, no technician, no heavy equipment hire.

**You do need a booth when:**

- the format is an auditorium with a stage and a PA;
- more than 30 people are listening to interpretation;
- the room is quiet and full, and a continuous background voice would intrude;
- the event runs three or more languages at once.

A booth acoustically isolates the interpreter. That is what it does, and it is
why it costs more: it brings physical structure, a sound desk, a technician and
receivers in quantity.

If your case is the second one, we say so in the quote and hire the equipment —
we do not push the portable system into a format it does not serve.
