question: Why do you always quote two interpreters?
order: 3
page: booth-or-portable
---

Because it is standard practice in the profession, not a commercial add-on.

Simultaneous interpreting demands sustained attention at a level that cannot be
held alone. After twenty to thirty continuous minutes, quality drops
measurably — omissions increase, numbers come out wrong, sentence construction
gets thinner. This happens to every interpreter, including very good ones.

So the pair rotates in short shifts. And the interpreter who is “off” is not
idle: they write down figures, proper nouns and terminology for the one who is
live.

**The exception:** events of up to an hour, with nothing following, can be done
by one interpreter. If that is your case, we will suggest it ourselves — it is
cheaper for you and it does not compromise the result.

If someone quotes a full day with a single interpreter, the price will be
lower. The second half of the day will not be.
